## Custom Sunbrella Curtains
`/collections/custom-sunbrella-curtains` — 10 FAQs

**Q1. Can Sunbrella curtains be used outdoors?**

Absolutely. Sunbrella curtains are specifically engineered for outdoor applications. The solution-dyed acrylic fabric resists fading even with constant sun exposure, while water-repellent and mildew-resistant treatments protect against rain and humidity. They perform beautifully on covered patios, screened porches, pool houses, and any outdoor area where they're protected from extreme wind.

**Q2. How do I clean my Sunbrella curtains?**

Sunbrella curtains are machine-washable. Simply remove them from the rod, wash in cold water on a delicate cycle with mild detergent, and air dry. For spot cleaning, use mild soap and lukewarm water. For stubborn stains, apply a solution of 1 cup of bleach and ¼ cup mild soap per gallon of water, let soak for 15 minutes, rinse thoroughly, and air dry.

**Q3. How long do Sunbrella curtains last outdoors?**

With proper care, Sunbrella curtains maintain their color and performance for 10+ years in outdoor applications. Sunbrella's solution-dyed acrylic fibers are engineered for superior fade resistance and durability compared to conventional outdoor fabrics that deteriorate within 2-3 years.

**Q4. What header style should I choose for my curtains?**

Grommet headers work best for curtains that will be frequently opened and closed, offering a smooth gliding operation. Tab top and back tab styles suit stationary or occasionally adjusted curtains with a more relaxed aesthetic. Rod pocket headers create classic gathered pleats for a traditional look. Consider your rod type, desired appearance, and how often you'll adjust the curtains.

**Q5. How many curtain panels do I need for my window or opening?**

Each Sunbrella curtain panel is 50" wide. For a fuller, layered appearance, we recommend ordering 1.5 to 2 times your window width in total panel coverage. For example, a 100" wide opening would look best with 2-3 panels (100-150" total width). For stationary panels used purely for framing, one panel on each side may be sufficient.

**Q6. Do Sunbrella curtains block UV rays?**

Yes. Sunbrella fabrics block up to 98% of harmful UV rays, protecting your indoor furnishings from sun damage while creating comfortable, shaded spaces. This UV protection helps prevent fading of furniture, flooring, and artwork in sunrooms and rooms with large windows.

**Q7. Can I use Sunbrella curtains indoors?**

Definitely. While Sunbrella curtains excel in outdoor applications, they're equally beautiful and functional for indoor use. Sunbrella fabric is soft, breathable, and GREENGUARD Gold certified for low chemical emissions — a safe choice for kids’ rooms and high-traffic family areas. Many customers choose them for sunrooms, rooms with large windows, or anywhere superior fade resistance and easy-care maintenance are valued.

**Q8. Which Sunbrella fabric colors fade the least in the sun?**

All Sunbrella fabrics are solution-dyed, meaning color is locked into the fiber before it's formed, providing superior fade resistance across all colors. However, neutral tones like beige, taupe, gray, and natural shades typically show the least visible fading over time compared to vibrant colors, though even bright Sunbrella colors significantly outperform conventional outdoor fabrics.

**Q9. Should I take down my outdoor Sunbrella curtains during winter?**

While Sunbrella curtains are designed to withstand outdoor elements year-round, we recommend storing them during extended periods of non-use or severe winter weather to maximize their lifespan. If left outside, ensure they can dry completely between rain or snow events to prevent moisture accumulation.

**Q10. What makes Sunbrella fabric worth it for curtains?**

Solution-dyed construction. The color goes through every fiber instead of sitting on the surface, so Sunbrella curtains keep their color through 10+ years of sun, rain, and humidity — while conventional outdoor fabrics wash out in 2–3. Add machine-washability, bleach-cleanable stain removal, and up to 98% UV blocking, and one set of Sunbrella panels outlasts several rounds of ready-made replacements.

## Sheer Curtains

**Q1. Do sheer curtains give privacy at night?**

During the day, yes — they obscure the view in while letting light through. At night, once your interior lights are on, sheers offer little privacy on their own. Layer them with blackout or room-darkening panels for full nighttime cover.

**Q2. What’s the difference between sheer and semi-sheer curtains?**

Sheer (voile) fabric is the lightest and most see-through, for maximum light and that airy look. Semi-sheer is a slightly tighter weave that softens light and adds a bit more daytime privacy and structure.

**Q3. How much fabric width do sheer curtains need?**

For a full, gathered look, plan for about 2 to 2.5 times your window width in total panel width. Because ours are made to measure, we build the right fullness in for the size you give us.

**Q4. Do sheer curtains reduce sun fading?**

They help take the edge off direct sun, which can slow fading of furniture and floors, but they don’t block UV completely. For stronger protection, layer sheers with a lined panel.

**Q5. What length should sheer curtains be?**

Floor-length is the most popular for sheers, since the light fabric drapes beautifully; a small puddle on the floor adds a soft, relaxed feel. For kitchens or baths, a sill or apron length keeps them practical.

**Q6. Can sheer curtains be made to measure in any color?**

Yes. You choose the color and weave, and we cut each panel to your exact window with the fullness built in — no settling for a standard size on the shelf.

## Blackout Curtains
`/collections/blackout-curtains` — 6 FAQs

**Q1. Do blackout curtains block 100% of light?**

A good blackout panel blocks the vast majority of it. A little can still creep in around the edges, so for a truly dark room, size the panels wider and longer than the window and mount them to overlap the frame. Fit does most of the work here.

**Q2. What’s the difference between blackout and room-darkening curtains?**

Blackout curtains block nearly all light; room-darkening curtains dim the room but let some through. Choose blackout for full darkness, room-darkening for a softer, still-lit space.

**Q3. Do blackout curtains keep a room cooler in summer and warmer in winter?**

Yes. The dense, lined fabric slows heat moving through the window — heat out in summer, warmth in during winter. That’s the same reason they double as thermal curtains and can ease the load on your heating and cooling.

**Q4. What size blackout curtains do I need?**

Measure your window width and the drop you want, then add extra on both so the panels extend past the frame. Ours are made to measure, so you give us the finished size and we cut to it exactly — no rounding up to the nearest standard.

**Q5. Are blackout curtains a good choice for a nursery?**

They’re one of the most popular picks for nurseries. A dark room helps naps stick and bedtimes land earlier. And because the lining does the blocking, you can keep soft, light colors and still get full darkness.

**Q6. Are custom blackout curtains better than ready-made?**

Yes, for one simple reason: fit. Cut to your exact window, custom panels hang clean and close the edge gaps that let light slip past ready-made sizes. Better darkness, a sharper look, and your choice of fabric and color instead of whatever’s on the shelf.

## Living Room Curtains
`/collections/living-room-curtains` — 7 FAQs

**Q1. What color curtains work best in a living room?**

It depends on the job you want them to do. Neutrals — soft white, greige, warm gray — keep the space calm and flexible, and they won't fight your furniture or clash when you restyle later. For a statement, go a few shades deeper than your wall color for a tone-on-tone look, or pull a color from your rug or art so it reads deliberate. Two things worth weighing: in a small or low-light room, lighter shades keep it open, while deeper tones make a large room feel cozier; and if the window gets strong afternoon sun, a lined panel protects a bold color from fading.

**Q2. How wide should living room drapes be?**

Your panels should total about two to two-and-a-half times the window width. That extra fabric is what lets them gather into full folds and close completely, instead of stretching flat and skimpy across the glass. Measure your rod — not just the window — and leave room for the panels to stack off the glass when open, so they don't block light or the view. Because ours are made to measure, we build that fullness into your exact numbers.

**Q3. Should living room drapes touch the floor?**

Yes — floor-length is the most flattering choice for a living room. Short, sill-length panels stop partway down the wall, making the window and the room look undersized. You've got three finishes: just kissing the floor for a crisp, tailored look; a slight break for something softer; or a puddle for a formal, dramatic feel. Kissing the floor is the most practical day-to-day, since a puddle collects dust and gets underfoot with kids or pets.

**Q4. Can I layer sheers and drapes in the living room?**

Yes — and it's one of the highest-impact upgrades you can make. A single panel can't be airy at noon and dark at night, but a layered window can: light sheers close to the glass filter daylight and give daytime privacy, while heavier or room-darkening drapes in front close over them for glare control, privacy, or movie night. A double rod or track holds both. Layering also adds fullness, a little insulation, and softens sound — so the window looks finished, not flat.

**Q5. What are modern curtain ideas for a living room?**

Clean lines and simple fabrics. Choose a tidy header like ripple-fold or grommet, a solid or subtle-texture fabric, and floor-length panels that just kiss the floor. Keep the palette restrained — tone-on-tone with the wall reads seamless and current — and skip heavy swags, valances, and busy patterns. Hanging the rod high and wide keeps the look tall and uncluttered.

**Q6. Do curtains make a living room look bigger?**

Yes, if you hang them right. Mount the rod high — close to the ceiling — and wide, extending six to twelve inches past each side of the window, so the panels frame the glass instead of covering it. Floor-length fabric draws the eye up and down the full wall, and light or wall-matched colors keep everything feeling open. The trick is making the window look larger than it is, which in turn stretches the whole room.

**Q7. How far should curtain rods extend past the window?**

Extend the rod about six to twelve inches past each side of the window frame. That lets the panels stack off the glass when they're open, so they frame the window instead of blocking it — you get more daylight, and the window looks wider. Pair it with mounting the rod high, close to the ceiling, and the whole room reads taller. For very wide windows or sliders, add a center support so a long rod doesn't sag.

## Kitchen Curtains
`/collections/kitchen-curtains` — 6 FAQs

**Q1. What length should kitchen curtains be?**

Short — sill length, apron length, or cafe/tier styles. In a kitchen, length is a safety and practicality call, not just a style one: floor-length panels catch grease, drag through spills, and near a burner they're a genuine fire risk. Sill length ends right at the windowsill for a crisp look; apron length drops a few inches lower for more coverage. Over a sink, cafe curtains and tiers cover the lower pane so you can still reach the faucet, and a valance alone works when you want softness up top with full light and counter access below.

**Q2. What kind of curtains are best for a kitchen?**

The best kitchen curtains are short and cut from easy-clean fabric — tiers, cafe curtains, or sill-length panels in a washable weave. That combination beats the two things a kitchen throws at a window: mess and clutter. Short lengths stay clear of counters, the sink, and the stove; washable fabrics survive the grease and steam that wreck delicate materials. For a window right over the sink, lean cafe or tier; for one away from the work zone, a sill- or apron-length panel gives you more of a decorative moment.

**Q3. Are cafe curtains good for kitchens?**

Yes — cafe curtains are a classic kitchen choice for good reason. Covering only the lower half of the window gives you privacy at the sink or counter right where you need it, while the open top keeps the kitchen bright. Because they're short by design, they never drag into the sink or hang near the stove. Add a valance up top for a fuller look, or leave it open for maximum light.

**Q4. What fabric is easiest to clean for kitchen curtains?**

Lightweight, tightly woven, washable fabrics — cotton blends or performance weaves — are the easiest to keep clean in a kitchen. They wipe down when splattered and go straight in the machine for a full refresh, which is often needed near a stove or sink. Avoid heavy linings, dry-clean-only fabrics, and loose open weaves by the cooking zone; they trap grease and odors and are a chore to clean. If the window is close to the burner, ask about a flame-resistant fabric that's still washable.

**Q5. Should kitchen curtains match the cabinets or the walls?**

Tie them to one color already in the room — that's the simple rule. Echo the wall color and the curtains blend in and make the space feel bigger; pick up a cabinet or backsplash color, and they tie the room together as a deliberate accent. Match the cabinets for a built-in, seamless look; contrast the walls if you want the window to pop. In a busy kitchen, a solid keeps things calm, while a plain kitchen can take a pattern.

**Q6. Can I put curtains over the kitchen sink window?**

Yes — and short tiers or cafe curtains are the ideal choice there. They give you privacy at the sink without dangling into the basin, blocking the faucet, or soaking up water and going mildewed like a long panel would. Mount them to clear the faucet, choose a washable fabric since this window gets the most splatter, and keep them easy to take down for a quick wash.

## Velvet Curtains

**Q1. Are velvet curtains good for blocking light?**

Yes — velvet is naturally room-darkening because the fabric is so dense, so it dims daylight well on its own. Add a blackout lining if you want to block light completely and turn your curtains into blackout velvet curtains.

**Q2. Do velvet curtains keep a room warmer?**

The heavy, dense fabric adds a real layer over the window, which helps ease drafts and hold warmth in cooler months. It’s one reason people can’t stop looking for velvet curtains for living room, because it feels so cozy in living rooms and bedrooms.

**Q3. How do you clean velvet curtains?**

Velvet prefers gentle care: steam lightly to refresh the pile and release creases, and avoid heavy washing that can crush or mark the fabric. Follow the specific care guidance for your chosen velvet.

**Q4. Do velvet curtains help reduce noise?**

The weight and density of velvet soften echo and take the edge off outside noise, which is why it suits larger or media rooms. It won’t soundproof a space, but it does make it feel quieter.

**Q5. What rooms suit velvet curtains best?**

Living rooms, bedrooms, and formal or media spaces, where the warmth, depth, and weight all pay off. Velvet is usually too heavy and formal for kitchens or baths.

**Q6. Can velvet curtains be made to measure in any color?**

Yes. You choose the color and length, and we cut each panel to your window with the fullness and header suited to velvet’s weight, so it hangs full and even.

**Q1. What are cafe curtains, and how are they different from regular curtains?**

Cafe curtains, also known as half curtains, are short panels that cover only the lower half of a window, mounted on a rod partway up rather than at the top. Unlike full-length curtains, they screen the bottom of the window — where people can see in — while leaving the top open for light. That half-and-half coverage is the whole idea: privacy where you need it, daylight where you don't. The style takes its name from European cafes, where it kept diners private from the street without darkening the room.

**Q2. Do cafe style curtains give enough privacy at night?**

During the day, cafe curtains give solid privacy for the lower half of the window — enough to cover you at a sink or vanity. At night, though, with interior lights on, the open top half can let people see in. If nighttime privacy matters, pair the cafe curtain with a shade or blind you can lower over the top after dark, or choose a slightly higher mount. It's the trade-off for keeping the room bright by day.

**Q3. Are cafe curtains still in style?**

Yes — cafe curtains have swung back into style, especially in kitchens, bathrooms, and cottage- or farmhouse-leaning homes. Their casual, unfussy look fits the move away from heavy, formal window treatments. In a fresh fabric — a simple stripe, a small check, a clean linen-look solid — they read charming and current rather than dated. As always, it's the fabric and fit that make them feel modern, not the format itself.

**Q4. Do cafe curtains block light or keep a room bright?**

Cafe curtains keep a room bright — that's their main advantage. Because they only cover the lower half of the window, the entire top half stays open to daylight, even when you're fully covered at eye level. If you want to dim or fully block light too, add a separate shade or blind up top. On their own, cafe curtains are about privacy with light, not darkening.

**Q5. Do cafe curtains work on large or tall windows?**

Yes, though the proportions matter. On a tall window, cover roughly the lower third to half so the panel doesn't look stranded, and add a valance up top to balance the height. On a wide window, we size the panel to the full width with enough fullness to gather, or split it into a pair. Because they're made to measure, we scale the coverage to the window so it looks intentional, not undersized.

**Q6. Do cafe curtains need to be hemmed or trimmed to fit?**

Ready-made cafe half curtains often do — a fixed store size rarely matches your window, so you end up hemming the length or settling for the wrong coverage. Made-to-measure cafe curtains skip that entirely: we cut them to the exact portion of the window you want covered and finish them with proper hems, so there's nothing to trim or adjust. That precise coverage is the whole point of a cafe curtain, and it's hard to get from a one-size panel.

## Window Curtains

**Q1. How do I measure a window for curtains?**

Measure the width of your curtain rod and the drop from the rod down to your desired length. For a fuller look and more light when open, mount the rod above the frame and extend it past both sides.

**Q2. How high should I hang window curtains?**

Hang the rod several inches above the window frame — often close to the ceiling — to make the window and room feel taller. Extending it past the sides lets the panels clear the glass when open.

**Q3. What length should window curtains be?**

Floor-length suits living rooms and bedrooms; sill or apron length suits kitchens and baths. For floor-length, choose between just touching the floor for a tailored look or a slight puddle for a softer one.

**Q4. What curtain header style should I choose?**

Pinch pleat reads tailored and formal, grommet is modern and easy to slide, and rod pocket is simple and casual. Pick the one that matches the room’s style and how often you’ll open and close them.

**Q5. How wide should curtains be for my window?**

Total panel width should be about two to two-and-a-half times the window width so they look full and close fully. Made to measure, we build that fullness into the size you give us.

**Q6. Can you make curtains for odd-shaped or large windows?**

Yes. Bay, arch, extra-tall, and extra-wide windows are exactly where made-to-measure matters most, since standard sizes can’t cover them cleanly.

## Door Curtains
`/collections/door-curtains` — 6 FAQs

**Q1. Do door curtains help with drafts and heat?**

Yes — a door is one of the draftiest spots in a house, and a heavier curtain adds an insulating layer that takes the edge off. In winter, a lined or heavyweight panel slows the cold air seeping in around the frame; in summer, it blocks some of the heat and glare pouring through the glass. It won't seal a door like weatherstripping, but it noticeably softens the chill and can ease the load on your heating.

**Q2. Do curtains work on a door you open and close every day?**

Yes, as long as the header and hardware are built for it. A smooth-gliding header — grommet or rod-pocket — on a sturdy rod or track, sized so the panels clear the opening completely, moves out of the way with a single pull instead of something you fight each time. Made-to-measure sizing matters here, because a panel that's too wide or too long is the one that snags and wears out early.

**Q3. Can I match my door curtains to my window curtains?**

Yes, and it's worth doing. The same fabric, color, and header on the door and the nearby windows makes the doorway blend into the room instead of standing out as a separate treatment. It's the easiest way to make a patio slider or French doors feel designed rather than like an afterthought. Because ours are made to measure, we can match the look across a window and a door even though the two are completely different sizes.

**Q4. Are curtains or blinds better for a sliding glass door?**

It depends on the look and how you use the door, and plenty of homes use both. Curtains soften a big glass slider, add warmth and color, and stack fully clear when you want the view; vertical blinds are more utilitarian and tend to rattle or bend over time. If you want the door to feel like part of the room, curtains win; if you only need quick light control, blinds are simpler. A common combo is a curtain for softness with a shade behind it for precise control.

**Q5. Do you need curtains on a glass front or back door?**

If the door has clear glass, curtains are worth it for privacy and light control. A glass front or back door lets anyone see straight into your entryway or kitchen, and a fitted panel covers that sightline while you still open the door normally. Use a short rod-pocket panel mounted top and bottom for a single glass pane, or a full-length panel for a mostly-glass door. It also softens the harsh light a bare glass door throws across the floor.

**Q6. Can renters hang door curtains without drilling?**

Yes — for lighter panels, a tension rod or adhesive hooks let you hang door curtains without drilling into the frame, which is ideal for renters. Just know that heavier, full-length panels need properly mounted hardware to hang straight and take daily use, so match the panel weight to the mount. If drilling isn't an option, tell our custom curtain experts and we'll help you pick a lighter fabric and a no-drill setup that still fits your door.

Heavier, lined panels add a soft layer over the window that can take the edge off outside noise. They won’t soundproof a room, but they do help it feel quieter.

## Pinch Pleat Curtains
`/collections/pinch-pleat-curtains` — 9 FAQs

**Q1. What are pinch pleat curtains?**

Pinch pleated curtains have the fabric gathered and stitched into pleats along the top, so it falls in crisp, evenly spaced folds instead of hanging flat. It's the most tailored header you can put on a window — the reason the same fabric looks structured and finished rather than casual. Because the pleats depend on the right fullness, we build ours to measure for your exact window.

**Q2. Are pinch pleat curtains outdated?**

No. Pinch pleat drapes are timeless the way a well-cut suit is timeless — the look reads traditional or modern depending on the fabric and rod you pair with them. The tailored header is what designers reach for when they want a room to look finished, and that hasn't changed. It's a classic, not a trend.

**Q3. What's the difference between pinch pleat and grommet curtains?**

Pinch pleated curtains have sewn-in pleats and hang from rings or hooks, for a tailored, formal drape. Grommet curtains have metal-ringed holes that slide straight onto the rod, for a cleaner, more casual, modern look. Pinch pleats dress a room up; grommets keep it relaxed.

**Q4. What's the difference between double and triple pinch pleats?**

A double pleat gathers two folds per pleat, for a lighter, softer look. A triple pleat — also called a French pleat — gathers three, for a fuller, dressier drape. Triple pleats suit heavier fabrics and larger, more formal rooms; doubles feel a touch more relaxed. We'll help you match the pleat to your fabric and your room.

**Q5. How much fullness do pinch pleat curtains need?**

Enough fabric to hold full, even folds — usually about 2 to 2.3 times the width for a double pleat, and 2.5 to 3 times for a triple. Too little and they hang flat; too much and they crowd. For tailored pleat curtains, we calculate the right fullness for your exact width so the folds sit even from edge to edge.

**Q6. What hardware do pinch pleat curtains hang from?**

Two common ways: rings with hooks on a decorative rod, when you want the rod to show, or hooks on a traverse track, when you want them to glide open and closed and the hardware to disappear. The choice comes down to the look you want and how often you'll draw them. Either way, we set the tailored pleat curtains header to match your setup.

**Q7. Should pinch pleat curtains touch the floor?**

For the cleanest look, they should just kiss the floor — a hover of about half an inch above it. That's the tailored, designer finish, and it keeps the hem from bunching or catching. When you order, we set the drop to your exact measurement so the length lands right the first time.

**Q8. How do you clean pinch pleat curtains?**

Take them down by slipping the hooks out of the pleats, then follow the care guidance for your fabric — many can be gently machine washed cold and hung to dry, while heavier or lined panels do best with professional cleaning to keep the pleats sharp. In between, a quick vacuum with the brush attachment or a light steam on the hung panel keeps them fresh. Always check the fabric's care label first.

**Q9. Are French pleat curtains the same as pinch pleat curtains?**

French pleat curtains are a type of pinch pleat — specifically the triple pleat, where the fabric is gathered into three folds at the top instead of two. That extra fold is what gives French pleats their fuller, dressier drape, which is why they're the go-to for heavier fabrics and larger, more formal rooms. So every French pleat is a pinch pleat, but not every pinch pleat is a French pleat. When you order, we'll help you decide whether a double or a French (triple) pleat suits your fabric and your window best.

Solution-dyed acrylic — Sunbrella is the best known — is the top choice. The fire-resistant ones are made from flame-retardant fabric — either inherently flame-retardant fibers (fire-resistant at the fiber level) or fabric given a flame-retardant finish after weaving. **Q3. How do I know if my curtains are flame retardant?**

Check the label or spec sheet for a flame-test standard — in the US, drapery is rated to NFPA 701. Flame-retardant curtains ship with documentation naming the standard they meet; if no rating is listed, assume the panels aren't flame retardant. Every flame-retardant curtain we make is built to meet NFPA 701, and we can provide the paperwork your inspector or specifier needs.

**Q4. How to fireproof curtains?**

Strictly speaking, curtains can't be made fireproof — no fabric truly is — but they can be made flame retardant, which resists ignition and slows flame from spreading. That happens one of two ways: choose inherently flame-retardant fabric, or apply a certified flame-retardant treatment to existing panels. Not automatically. To count as fire retardant, polyester has to be inherently flame-retardant (fire resistance built into the fiber) or given a certified flame-retardant finish, then tested to NFPA 701. The NFPA 701 rating is a one-time test of the fabric, so it doesn’t carry an expiration date.
